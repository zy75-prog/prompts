Study radiation tolerance of a material I specify using MLIPs on NOTS.

Workflow:
1. Obtain and verify its Materials Project structure.
2. Relax the pristine primitive cell's lattice and atomic positions at zero
   pressure.
3. Use supercell dimensions I specify, or propose a small initial supercell
   based on the relaxed structure and available resources. Report its
   dimensions and atom count for approval, then relax atomic positions with
   the cell fixed.
4. Search for isolated interstitial ground-state and low-energy
   configurations of each species, without a vacancy.
5. Evaluate threshold displacement energies for each species using fixed-cell
   recoil MD.
6. Calculate Frenkel-pair recombination barriers using fixed-cell defect
   relaxation and climbing-image NEB.

After relaxing the pristine bulk lattice, keep supercell lattice vectors fixed
while allowing atomic positions to relax or evolve. Use the same bulk cell for
defect endpoints and all NEB images. For finite-temperature recoil MD,
equilibrate the pristine bulk at the target temperature and pressure first,
then fix the cell during recoil. Keep the approved supercell size unless I
authorize a change; report finite-size limitations.

For displacement thresholds, adapt the recoil protocol in the reference below
using MLIP forces. Start from relaxed bulk at 0 K with zero velocities, give
one selected atom recoil kinetic energy in a specified direction, and run
fixed-cell NVE MD. Test inequivalent sites and multiple directions. Run 1 ps,
then relax atomic positions; the paper uses a 0.03 eV/angstrom force tolerance.
If the crystal heals, raise recoil energy in 5 eV steps, then refine the first
defect-producing interval in 1 eV steps. Check nearby energies for nonmonotonic
defect survival rather than assuming a single sharp threshold.

Confirm each candidate threshold with a separate 2 ps run from the same
pristine recoil initial conditions, followed by relaxation. Require defects
to survive both durations. Check timestep convergence and total-energy drift;
the paper's 1 fs timestep is a reference, not an automatic MLIP setting.
Report direction-specific threshold brackets and the minimum found among
sampled directions; distinguish these from directional averages. A 1 eV scan
resolution does not establish total accuracy, especially for an unvalidated
collision potential. Retain recoil-generated defects for recombination NEB.

Before classifying defects, align the relaxed structure to pristine bulk to
remove rigid translation under periodic boundaries. Use species-aware atom
matching and site occupancies to identify vacancies, interstitials, antisites,
and replacement chains. Distinguish identical-atom exchanges from surviving
defects. Check healing with aligned structure, occupancies, and energy, not
the primary recoil atom's displacement alone.

For isolated interstitials, add one atom to the approved pristine supercell.
Sample octahedral and tetrahedral voids, other open sites, split-interstitial
orientations, and randomly perturbed configurations. Relax atomic positions,
group equivalent final structures, rank distinct minima within each species at
identical composition, and expand sampling until the low-energy ranking
stabilizes. Retain distinct low-energy metastable states as well as the
ground-state candidate, reporting their relative energies and structures. Use
prior findings as checks, not restrictions. Label the ground state as the
lowest-energy state found under the chosen model and cell, not a proven
global minimum.

Use the distinct low-energy interstitial configurations to seed Frenkel pairs;
also retain defects generated by recoil MD. Sample close, intermediate, and
farther vacancy–interstitial separations within the approved supercell. Report
initial and relaxed shortest periodic distances, pair energies, and whether
pairs heal or remain as defects. Separate geometry effects from distance
effects where possible. Run NEB only for pairs that survive sufficiently tight
relaxation; consider successive migration hops through independently relaxed
intermediate states for farther pairs.
